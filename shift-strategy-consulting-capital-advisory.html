The Shift From Strategy Consulting to Capital Advisory

📅 June 15, 2026
✎ Bainbridge Capital Research Team
⌚ 6 min read

A strategy memo and a term sheet solve different problems—but the best ones are built on the same honest research.

For twenty-five years, Bainbridge operated purely as a strategy consultancy, born out of MIT in 1975 and built to help executives navigate growth and competitive strategy. The work was analytical, research-heavy, and advisory: firms hired Bainbridge to tell them what the evidence actually said, then went and acted on it themselves.

Why Advice Alone Wasn't Enough

By the late 1990s, a pattern had become obvious inside the firm's engagements. Clients who took Bainbridge's strategic recommendations into an M&A process or a capital raise often struggled in the execution phase—not because the strategy was wrong, but because strategy and capital markets execution were being handled by two different advisors who didn't share context, and sometimes didn't share incentives either.

Bainbridge's answer, launched in 2000, was a capital advisory practice built on the same research discipline and the same standard of transparency as the strategy work—just pointed at deals instead of memos.

“Strategy without execution is a well-researched opinion.”

What Changed in Practice

The capital advisory practice didn't replace the strategy work—it gave it somewhere to go. Today that means guiding clients through M&A transactions, recapitalizations, and growth financings using the same primary-source research and proprietary data sourcing that has defined the firm since 1975, always with an eye toward value that lasts well beyond the closing date.

It also meant the firm could finally close the loop between advisory and execution. That integration is most visible in how Bainbridge now pairs sell-side and buy-side advisory within a single engagement, rather than handing clients off between specialists.

A Full-Service Investment Bank, Research-First

Two and a half decades later, Bainbridge Capital is a full-service investment bank serving business owners, capital groups, and Fortune 500 companies. The firm has been named a Forbes Best Management Consulting Firm every year from 2016 to 2026, and Vault.com ranks it among the most prestigious consulting firms in North America.

None of that changes what the firm actually is at its core, which you can read more about on the Bainbridge Capital about page: a research shop that happens to close deals, rather than a deal shop that happens to do research. Learn more about Bainbridge Capital's website for current engagements.

Conclusion

The shift from strategy consulting to capital advisory wasn't a pivot away from Bainbridge's roots—it was what those roots were always pointed toward. Research earns its keep when it survives contact with a live transaction, and when it leaves clients with value they can build on for years.
